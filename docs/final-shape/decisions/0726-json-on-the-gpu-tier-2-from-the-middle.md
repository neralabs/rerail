# 0726: JSON on the GPU: tier 2 from the middle, then the tape, LZ4 and UKL

Status: in progress (2026-11-27). The user's: "I want to get the JSON parsing on the GPU via bitlex", every stage "in
ukl and all driven by the bitlex grammar". The design is the page the user reviewed (version 2), with the user's
corrections: the lexer from the middle is the one already built, and tier 2's chunked form is JSON's own.

## Intro

The CPU reads JSON with the Bitlex compiler's generated Rust in two tiers (`json_aot`): tier 1, simdjson's stage 1
as masks, finds each token's first byte; tier 2, one loop, walks the records and writes format 16. The GPU form
needs each stage to run on every piece of the file at once:

1. token boundaries and types, and the token indexes by type;
2. every token processed at once;
3. the parse's superposition, chunk by chunk;
4. a prefix sum that gives the actual parse and the node numbers;
5. the tape written in parallel;
6. LZ4 of every block's pieces in parallel (16z).

Stage 1 exists: tier 1's three passes (`stage1_ends`, `entries`, `stage1_from`, generated by `bitlex_lex_rust`,
commit 39b2faa6fd). The user (2026-11-27): "you have already done a middle-out version of the lexer. Find it and use
it, don't re-invent anything". LZ4: "using the greedy one is fine, we can do the hc one later".

## Decision

### 1. Tier 2's chunked form is JSON's own, from its loop

The user (2026-11-27), on how to hold tier 2's nest (1,024 levels deep): "We are doing JSON, not Kotlin, rr and
Python. … We just need to know enough to superpose the states at the beginning and end of the blocks. We will also
need a way for a block to close more than it opens. So that we can resolve the blocks parse state with prefix sums."

So the token loops' core (`bitlex_lex_rust::chunked`, 0715) is unchanged. `bitlex_rust/one_loop/chunked.rs` makes
tier 2's form from its loop's text (`run`), appended to the tier:

- **A chunk starts after a separator** (`,`, the lists' `SEPS`), where the loop is at a list's cycle place
  (`CYCLES`: an object's next pair, 2, or an array's next element, 5). Those are the superposition of a chunk's
  start. The first chunk starts at the root's place.
- **The walk from any place** (`run_range`): the serial loop with an exit at each place at or past the chunk's end
  (the `'run` and `'ret` loops' heads, and each list's `continue 'lN` made place `N`), its refusals and its end a
  `Stop`, and through `Out` (the writer's cursor, or `Count`, the nodes counted).
- **A chunk may close more than it opens: its closers are anti-opens** (the user, 2026-11-27: "closing should be
  implemented as accumulating anti-opens as a mirror operation, not as something different"; the design page
  "Closing as anti-opens"). An open pushes a return place that is a fact of the level it is in, not of its opener:
  the root's 7, an object's 3, an array's 6 (`CONT`, by the level's kind, the root last), and a chunk's first record
  is at its innermost level's cycle place (`CYCLE`: an object's 2, an array's 5). The generator derives both from
  the loop's text: a return place's arm takes its kind's closer (the arm with none is the root's), and its separator
  goes to the kind's cycle place; a grammar where an arm takes two kinds' closers, or a kind lacks either, is refused.
- **The bracket scan** (`kinds`): each chunk's word (`Word::of`) is its unmatched closers' kinds in file order (its
  anti-opens) and the openers it leaves open; two words compose by cancelling the second's anti-opens against the
  first's opens, innermost first (`Word::then`, the bracket monoid), a mismatched pair breaking the word. Down its
  tree each chunk gets the open levels' kinds at its first record (`kinds_after`), kept to its anti-opens and the
  innermost one (`kinds_keep`). It reads each record's first byte only, as `depths` does, before any walk.
- **One walk a chunk** (`span`): from `CYCLE` of its innermost kind, its levels' places `CONT` of the kinds its
  anti-opens close, so it never reads a level it does not know and never forks. What it leaves is a span of the
  writer's frames (`Span`): the frames it closes below its entry and what it writes into them (its anti-frames,
  `fkc`, `touch`), those it leaves open (`pf`), its nodes, its last start, its exit or end. The forked form it
  replaces walked each chunk once for each of its start places and each place a closed level could return to, and
  folded the lanes and their refusals; in a nest 1,023 deep its summaries held 513 lanes before the folding.
- **The refusal is the first chunk that ended.** The chunk holding the first bracket mismatch has the serial walk's
  entry (every bracket before it is the serial walk's levels), so its walk refuses as the serial walk does; the
  frame scan goes on from a span that ended as itself, so it never reaches a chunk past it with no kinds.
- **The depth is a prefix sum** of each record's opener and closer (`depths`), a fact of the bytes, so a walk's depth
  is the file's and the loop's `DEPTH` test is the serial one. A chunk deeper than `DEPTH` is past a refusal.
- **Pass 3** (`entries`) is Blelloch's work-efficient scan, for both scans (`tree_of`, `down`): the values' tree up,
  a node the composition of two below; then the states down it (the root's the file's start; a left child's its
  parent's, a right child's its parent's through its left sibling's value). The frame scan (`Span::then`,
  `at_after`) gives each chunk its first node number, its open frames and the start before it; each chunk's levels'
  places are then `CONT` of its kinds. Hillis and Steele's scan, first, made every chunk's prefix a value in every
  round: in the 1,023-deep nest its last rounds held the square of the chunks.
- **A state holds what its range reads** (`kinds_keep`, `at_keep`): as many innermost levels as its chunk closes
  below its start and as many frames as it closes and then writes into, not the whole nest. Pass 2 places the levels
  at their depths.

The helpers that write (`open`, `leaf`, `leaf_in`, `token`, `close`, `string`, `value_primitive`) are generic over
`Out`. The serial walk calls them with the cursor, as before.

### 2. Pass 2: the tape from each chunk's entry

The writer's cursor holds the frames of the open nodes (node, children so far, last child, start) and makes and
settles blocks in node order. So a chunk's span carries what its range does to them, its anti-frames the mirror of
its frames, as its word carries the nest's brackets:

- the frames it closes of its entry's (`fkc`), what it writes into those (`touch`: children added, the last), and the
  frames it leaves open (`pf`), their nodes from the range's first; the last node's start (`last_start`). `Shadow` is
  the `Out` that keeps them as the cursor does, without writing.
- `then` and `apply` compose them with the nest, so pass 3 gives each chunk its first node, the start before it and
  its open frames (`At::frames`, `At::prev_start`).
- A cursor from the middle (`writer16::Cursor<X, true>`, `Cursor::resume`) writes a chunk's nodes from them: every
  block is made first (`Writer::make_blocks`, the node count from pass 3), none is settled while chunks write
  (`finish` makes each final, in block order), and the state's fields are set once at the end (`end_middle`). The
  serial cursor (`MID` false, the default) is unchanged.
- `parse_middle` runs the three passes; `write_range` is one chunk's pass 2.

Each cell has one writer, the chunk of its node, but for a node's slot, which the chunk that writes the node after
writes, and an open node's length and size, which the chunk that closes it writes. Each is written once.

**The side lists by a count.** A value past its cell's escape (and a code past the cut) is an entry of its block's
side list, kept in row order, which the serial writer inserts. From the middle, the chunks run twice (the user,
2026-11-27: "please build these: that count"):

- counting: each entry's row marked in its list's mask, one of `Z` bits a list at the end of each block's side area
  (`SIDE_MASKS`; `SIDE_AREA` grows by them, in the CLIF ops, `writer16` and `<ukl/writer.h>`), by an atomic or;
- writing: each entry at its rank among the mask's rows (`State::mid_entry`), so every entry has its one place
  whatever order the chunks write in; `end_middle` sets each list's count from its mask.

A cell's value is the same in both runs, so the cells may be written in both.

### 3. The records of each type: tier 1's pass 2 run twice

The user (2026-11-27): "please build these: … the per-type token indexes". The types are the grammar's tokens (`'"'`,
`punctuation`, `number`), each the mask of its `start`; `bitlex_lex_rust` keeps them (`Gen::types`) as it makes the
records' mask from their union, and their masks live in pass 2 alone (the serial `stage1` is unchanged).

- `stage1_from` runs twice from each chunk's entry: counting (`write` false), the chunk's records and each type's as
  popcounts of the masks; then writing, each record at its index in the file and each type's records' numbers at
  their index among the type's, from places an exclusive scan of the counts gives (`lex_typed`, `Typed`).
- A chunk writes only its own places: one write a bit (`write_exact`, `write_numbers`), not simdjson's writes past
  the count, which would reach the next chunk's records.
- `lex_chunked` is `lex_typed`'s records.

### 4. Stage 2: every token on its own

The token pass (`tokens`) reads each token of the primitive's (`value`'s alternatives that call no level: strings,
words, numbers) from each type's records, in any order, each one on its own (`token_at`): the primitive run at the
token's record through `Tok`, an `Out` that keeps its first node's number (the primitive's alternatives numbered 0..),
its nodes and the last one's start, or the byte the token is refused at. The records it reads are those whose first
byte is one of the primitive's arms' (`TOKEN_FIRSTS`, from the arms' patterns).

Every helper that takes a token's record (`string`, the primitive) asks its `Out` first (`known`): pass 1's `Shadow`
answers from the token pass and adds the token's nodes as one child of the open node (`skip`), so pass 1 reads no
token's bytes. The writer's cursor answers `None`, so pass 2 writes each token's nodes as the serial walk does, and the
oracle (`serial_entries`) reads each token itself.

### 5. The greedy LZ4 encoder

`ukl/lz4_encode.h` (`ukl_lz4_encode_sg`): a block encoded by one SIMD group, LZ4's fast parse. The walk is the
group's, in step: from its place the lanes take a window of positions, a position a lane; a position's candidate is
the latest earlier one with its word's hash (the nearest lane below with an equal hash, by `ukl_sg_match`, else the
table's, in threadgroup memory); the first lane whose candidate holds its word within 64 KB starts the match (a
ballot), every position up to it goes in the table, and the walk goes on past the match. So the parse is the serial
one at every width and on every backend: each position looked up and then put in the table, the first match taken,
the positions inside a match skipped. A match is measured a byte a lane, literals copied a byte a lane, and LZ4's
limits hold (no match in the last 12 bytes, the last 5 literals), so LZ4's own decoder takes the block.

**The 16z pieces** (S6). A tape from the middle has every block made first and none final while chunks write, so
its 16z is made from the finished format 16 tape (`f16::z_of`), every piece on its own: block `b`'s piece `q` is read
where it lies (array `q`'s column at the block's rows, then side list `q`'s part `b`), and stored LZ4 where that saves
a tenth, else raw (no repeats, which the reader does not need). The tail is the tape's regions (its part starts, its
checkpoints in node order, its newline index), then the piece index. `Tape16Z::from_bytes` takes it as the CPU
writer's.

On the device a SIMD group encodes a piece from its two places (`ukl_lz4_encode2_sg`, `UklLz4Spans`), nothing joined:
once counting (`write` false: its length alone), then, after a scan of the lengths, written at its place. The
encoder's room check is exact, so a piece given the room its count found fits it. `f16::lz4_greedy` is the serial
parse, the one reference the device and `z_of` are held to.

### 6. Tier 1's passes as UKL: a printer of its Rust's lines

Tier 1's passes are written in a narrow form: words of 64 bits a block, the 64-byte blocks' operations of
`rerail_simd8::portable` (`s::`), loops over the blocks and the lanes, and a few shapes of their own (a lane met, a
lane's end, the pass's verdict). `bitlex_lex_rust::ukl::tier1` reads its functions (`lane_carries`, `write_exact`,
`write_numbers`, `stage1_ends`, `stage1_from`) line by line and prints each line as UKL: its expressions parsed and
printed with every operation in parentheses (Rust's `&` binds tighter than `==`; C's does not), each binding's type
from its value, a name bound again in its own scope assigned. A line it does not know is an error, so the form stays
closed: a change to tier 1 the printer does not take fails `json_rust_is_current`, never a kernel. `<ukl/blx64.h>` is
the block's operations as `s::` defines them, on a block of eight 64-bit words (SWAR): a byte is reached by a shift of
its word, and a 16-byte table is read once into two words, each byte's entry shifted out (a shuffle in registers). A
first form, a 64-byte array a block, ran right on the CPU and the M1 Max, but the M3's back end read the bytes written
into it back with each pair swapped (byte `i` as `i ^ 1`; a probe of the first block's masks showed it), whatever the
indexes' masks (decision 0540's fix): the words are the GPU's own form of a block, and run right on both.

The kernels are printed with it, a thread a chunk: `json_t1_ends` (pass 1, each lane's end) and `json_t1_from` (pass
2, counting, or writing each record at its index and each type's records' numbers into one buffer, each type's list
after the types before it). `json_aot/src/tier1.uglykl` is the printer's, held to it by `json_rust_is_current`
(`TIER1_UKL`).

The token pass (stage 2, section 4) is printed the same way (`bitlex_lex_rust::ukl::tier2_tokens`): the
primitive's helpers (`string`, `value_primitive`, the number's check, their tables), each walk helper printed once for
each `Out` it runs with (here the pass's `Tok`), a `Result<usize, u32>` as a struct (`?` its test hoisted before the
line), a `match` as tests in turn (C's `switch` would take `break`), and the `known` short cut dropped for an `Out` that
keeps no answers. Its kernel `json_t2_tokens` reads a token a thread, at each record of tier 1's type lists as
`json_t1_from` writes them.

Pass 2 is printed with it, in one program (`json_aot/src/tier2.uglykl`, `TIER2_UKL`): the walk from a place
(`run_range`) and its helpers, for the writer's cursor from the middle, `<ukl/writer_mid.h>`: `writer16`'s
`Cursor<X, true>` op for op. Chunks write next to each other in one block, so each cell is stored at its width alone
(a byte at a time), and a side-list mask's 64-bit word takes two 32-bit atomic ors (Metal has none of 64 bits). A
labelled loop's `continue` past the innermost loop sets a jump word and leaves each loop up to the one it names (Metal
has no `goto`). Its kernel `json_t2_write` runs a chunk a thread from its state: its frames copied into the chunk's
room, as `resume` writes them, its levels into its row of the levels' room.

The bracket scan and pass 1 are printed with them. `json_t2_words` makes each chunk's word, a thread a chunk, from
its records' first bytes (`json_t2_bracket`, from the Rust's `bracket`); the words' scan runs on the scan kernels
below. `json_t2_spans` is a chunk's one walk, a thread a chunk: the walk and its helpers again, for `Shadow`
(`{p}Sh`, the writer's frames kept without writing, the token pass's answers read through its `known`), from the
place and levels its kinds give (`json_t2_cycle`, `json_t2_cont`). Every value is a record of words in a pool taken
by an atomic bump (`<ukl/middle.h>`'s layouts: a word, kinds, a span, a state); a chunk's frames and its writes into
its entry's are placed by its first record and its number, so no chunk's room meets another's. A value past the pool
is `FULL`, and the run is made again with more.

Pass 3 is one set of scan kernels for both scans (`json_t2_up`, `json_t2_root`, `json_t2_down`, `op` naming the
scan): the tree up, a level a launch, a node the composition of two below (`ukl_word_then`, `ukl_span_then`), the
last of an odd level its child's offset (the same record, not a copy of it); the file's start at the top and the
file's end through the root; then the states down it, a level a launch, a right child's through its left sibling
(`ukl_kinds_after`, `ukl_at_after`), each as its range reads it (`ukl_kinds_keep`, `ukl_at_keep`). `json_t2_levels`
puts each chunk's levels' places into its state. A state no scan meets is `NONE`, one past the room `FULL`. The
launches are each tree's height twice, plus three: the phases on one persistent grid make them a fixed count.

Pass 2 reads each chunk's state where pass 3 left it in the pool (`json_t2_write`): its frames into the chunk's room
(placed by its first record and its number, as pass 1's), its levels into its row. The blocks are made on the device
before it (`json_t2_blocks`, `<ukl/writer_mid.h>`'s `ukl_wmid_make_block`, a thread a block: a fresh format 16
writer's block `b` is at a place of `b` alone, so every block at once, as many as the file's end state's nodes fill),
and the tape ended after it (`json_t2_ended`: each block's side lists counted from their masks, the state's nodes and
last start the end state's). So passes 1, 3 and 2 run with nothing read by the host between them, in one command
buffer. The host sets the writer's room before (`reserve_run`, for the most nodes the file's records and bytes can
have) and finishes the tape after (`finish`).

### 7. Every chunk on its own, in any order

The user (2026-11-27): "run the blocks in random order (including the prefix sum). This should allow us to validate the
shape." Every pass of both tiers runs its chunks in the order an `Order` gives (a permutation asked each time a pass
runs them), and each round of every scan too (`each`, `scan`: Hillis and Steele's, its two buffers apart):

- tier 1: pass 1 (`stage1_ends`), pass 3 (`entries_in`), pass 2's count, the counts' exclusive scan, and pass 2's
  writes (`lex_typed_in`);
- tier 2: the chunks' starts (each run of `size` records finds its own first record after a separator, placed by a
  prefix sum of the runs that have one), the depths (each chunk's sum of openers and closers, then their scan), pass
  the bracket scan (`kinds`), pass 1 and pass 3 (`entries`), and pass 2's count and writes (`parse_middle`).

The tests run them in seeded random orders, a new permutation at each ask: the answers are the serial ones. So no
chunk reads what another wrote but through a scan's rounds, and the shape is the device's: one launch a pass or a
scan round, each chunk a thread or a group.

### 8. Two programs, a launch a phase, the tape finished and stored as 16z on the device

The user (2026-11-27): "Chunk starts and depths computed on the device. The passes run with a fixed number of launches:
today they take each tree's height twice plus three. The tape finished on the device, then the LZ4 encoding of its
blocks." Built as two programs:

- `<ukl/middle_run.h>`: every pass of tier 2 from tier 1's records and the token pass's answers to the finished format
  16 tape, in eleven phases. RUNS: each run's first record after a separator, a leaf of the runs' scan. STARTS: each
  chunk's start placed. WORDS: each chunk's bracket word and depth change, leaves of the bracket and depth scans.
  KINDS: each chunk's kinds and depth. SPANS: each chunk's one walk, a leaf of the spans' scan, whose root says the
  verdict, the nodes and the blocks. STATES: each chunk's state, and the tape's blocks made. COUNT and WRITE: pass 2.
  END: the blocks ended, the last to end checking the state (`ended`). FIN: each block final (presence by atomics), a
  leaf of the blocks' scan, whose root lays the tail. LAY: each block's side lists, part starts, faults and presence
  words where they go, and the newline index.
- `<ukl/z16_run.h>`: the finished tape stored as 16z where it lies, in two phases. COUNT: each piece counted by a SIMD
  group's greedy parse (stopping at nine tenths of its bytes), the last to arrive laying the tape out (a scan of the
  lengths, the head, the piece index). WRITE: each piece at its place with its block's checkpoints, and the tail's
  part starts and newline index, 4 KB an item.

A phase is a launch, and its items are taken by the launch's SIMD groups by ticket from the count the phases before
it set (the user, 2026-11-27, of the options: "A launch per phase"). So the launches are a fixed eleven and two, and
every phase has the whole grid. A scan's tree goes up by arrivals within its leaves' phase (the second child composes
the parent, the root stores the state at the top and after the file). A later phase gives each leaf its state by
walking down from the top to it, each step the down-sweep's (a left child its parent's state, a right one its
parent's after its left sibling's value, each kept to what the child reads), so no level waits for another.

The first form, one persistent dispatch on a work queue whose workers end when they find it empty (decision 0540 step
7), ran a phase that starts from one item (a root, a fan's first item) on the one SIMD group still running: on the M3
Max, 2 MB of JSON took 587 ms of GPU in tier 2 and 78 ms in 16z, whatever the groups.

The phases are three kernels of `<ukl/middle_run.h>`, each its phases a constant mask so it holds their code alone:
pass 1's walks (`json_t2_run_walk`), pass 2's (`json_t2_run_write`, its count and writes one walk), and the rest
(`json_t2_run_scan`). Compiled cold on the M3 Max (a load average of 10): 0.6 s, 0.9 s and 16.4 s, every kernel 17.9 s.
A kernel a phase compiled pass 2's walk twice (19 and 20 s); one kernel of every phase took 39 to 67 s.

The host's side is `Writer::reserve_device_finish` (room for the tail) and `Writer::finished_on_device` (the tape taken
from the region as the device left it, no op run).

### 10. The whole chain from the bytes on the device

The user (2026-11-27): "which part of parsing JSON on the GPU meant parsing JSON on the CPU?" The timings above fed
tier 2 tier 1's records and the token pass's answers computed on the host. Now nothing but the source and its
length goes to the device:

- `<ukl/lex_run.h>` (`json_t1_lex`), tier 1 in three phases, a launch each, the scans of `lex_typed_in` on the
  device. ENDS: pass 1, each chunk's end lane from each entry lane, a map, a leaf of the maps' scan. COUNT: each
  chunk's entry (the maps before it composed, walked down from the top, applied to the file's start lane), pass 2
  counting, a leaf of the counts' scan, whose root says the totals and ends the records. WRITE: each chunk's places
  (walked down; each type's list after the types before it), pass 2 writing the records and the types' records, and
  each of its records' token none.
- The token pass (`json_t2_tokens`), a thread a typed record, as many as tier 1's pool says.
- Tier 2's kernels read the records and the refusal from tier 1's pool's header (`<ukl/lex_pool.h>`); a refused file
  gives every phase no items.

The host sizes every buffer by the file's length alone (a file of `n` bytes has at most `n + 1` records), so the
rooms are bounds, not the records' counts.

### 11. Rooms, wall time, the GPU's counters, batches, and 16z's matches (2026-11-27)

The user, after §10: size for the average case times two and a bit, not the worst; keep the buffers (pinned) and
never zero them; past a room, bail with the memory actually needed and run again. Then find what the wall time is,
look at what the GPU does with Instruments, fill the GPU, and look at suffix arrays and the matrix units for 16z.

**The wall time** was the host. Each run made about 1 GB of new buffers. Tier 2's pool alone, 537 MB, was built on
the host and copied in, and the writer's 338 MB region was built and copied twice: 240–300 ms of a 270–360 ms wall.

- The buffers are kept from file to file (`Kept`), grown by a quarter only when a file needs more, never zeroed.
  A run writes its header and layout. The writer's region gets the state and each part's bytes in use
  (`device_region_into`), not its room, since the writer's ops write before they read. The tests poison every new
  buffer with 0xa5 (`RERAIL_T2_POISON`) and stay byte-exact.
- The trees' arrivals exchange a per-run epoch into a buffer that only ever holds epochs (`ukl_run_meet`,
  `ukl_lex_meet`): the second arrival finds it there, so no counter is reset between runs.
- The rooms are twice the average so far and a bit: tier 1's records (0.3 a byte before any file), tier 2's pool
  (half a word a record and 350 a run, scaled by what runs used). Tier 1 past its room says the records it needs
  (`UKL_LEX_SHORT`) and writes nothing; tier 2's pool counts the words it took past its room. The host runs again
  with what they said. The writes test starts from averages far too small and checks the bails happen.
- The node room is the records plus the tokens' nodes, summed by the token pass a SIMD group at a time (it had run
  one thread a group), and checked on the device.
- One writer is kept too. With all of it, bytes to 16z on the M3 Max: 8.0 ms of wall, 15 µs of it the host's
  before tier 2.

**The counters.** The M3 Max and the M5 refuse Instruments' GPU counters ("Selected counter profile is not
supported"); the M1 Max gives them. One 2 MB file ran at 2–6% compute occupancy, 3–6% ALU, under 10% bandwidth.
The last 5.3 ms of tier 2's 14.5 ms ran at 0.6% occupancy: FIN set three device-wide atomic ORs a node into the
document's presence words, the same few words for every block. A block now gathers its bits in registers and ORs
each word once: FIN 2.8 → 1.8 ms at 16 records a chunk, 2.5 → 0.8 at 64, 2.0 → 0.5 at 256 (M3 Max).

**Batches.** The device's queue has concurrent compute encoders and barriers (`set_concurrent`, `barrier`), and
`batch` launches each phase of every file, then a barrier. Each file keeps its own buffers.
`json_batch_gives_each_files_tape` checks each file's tape and 16z, twice over kept buffers. On the M3 Max
(`json_batch_timings`), against Bitlex's CPU path to 16z on every core, a file a thread, each thread's writer kept:

| files | the device, wall | GPU | the CPU's cores |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 of 2 MB | 241–320 MB/s | 292–353 MB/s | 235–243 MB/s (1 thread) |
| 2 of 2 MB | 492 MB/s | 542 MB/s | 453 MB/s |
| 8 of 2 MB | 698–745 MB/s | 772–813 MB/s | 1,196–1,321 MB/s |
| 16 of 2 MB | 765 MB/s | 812 MB/s | (one run of 183 MB/s, an outlier, not rerun) |
| 256 of 64 KB | 448–522 MB/s | 552–652 MB/s | 1,454–2,156 MB/s |

The device saturates near 800 MB/s from 8 files on; 256 to 2,048 groups a launch change nothing. The counters with
8 files (M1 Max) say why. Occupancy stays at 14–18%, though every kernel allows 1,024 threads a group, so registers
are not the bound. The walks (pass 2's count and write) are latency-bound: one lane a chunk, serial and divergent,
8% ALU. One stretch runs at 58% MMU limiter and 78% write bandwidth: scattered writes over about 650 MB of buffers a
file miss the TLB. Those are the shape of tier 2, not its launch: the next gains are a walk data-parallel by record,
not by chunk, and buffers a few times the tape, not a hundred.

**16z's matches** (`json_16z_match_finders`, the 2 MB file's tape of 2,678,063 bytes):

| encoder | 16z bytes | CPU |
|---|---|---|
| the device's greedy parse (`lz4_greedy`) | 467,904 | 2.6 ms |
| LZ4's fast parse | 467,668 | 1.0 ms |
| LZ4 HC, level 9 | 452,500 | 7.7 ms |
| LZ4 HC, level 12 (the optimal parse) | 451,748 | 32.6 ms |
| one LZ4 stream for the whole tape, fast | 294,901 | |
| one LZ4 stream for the whole tape, HC 12 | 270,541 | |

The best a match finder could do within a piece, a suffix array or the matrix units among them, is 3.5%. Matches
across pieces are worth 37%. So a suffix array pays only over bytes shared by the pieces (a dictionary for the
tape, built once and searched by every piece at once), and that is a change to 16z's format. The matrix units
multiply small float matrices; finding byte matches in them would spend a 256-wide one-hot product on each
comparison a SIMD group's ballot makes in one instruction, so they are not a fit.
