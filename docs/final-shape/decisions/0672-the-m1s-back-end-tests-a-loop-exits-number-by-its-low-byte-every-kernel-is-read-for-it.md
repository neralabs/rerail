# 0672. The M1's back end tests a loop exit's number by its low byte: every kernel is read for it, and kept under it

Status: accepted (2026-11-24). Adds to 0571 (a split's units) and 0576 §22 (`UKL_LARGE` per backend): on the Apple G13
a large body is a call. Fixes the preprocessor's evaluation on the M1s (0653, 0613).

## Intro

Ten of the preprocessor's 47 evaluation tests failed on the M1 Max and the M1 (Mac mini) on Metal. All 47 passed on
the M3 and on the CPU. The cause was in Apple's GPU compiler for the G13 (the M1 family), not in our code. A
function past a size the compiler handles wrongly runs wrong, with no error from the compiler.

The user, 2026-11-24: record it, and "have something that will catch if there are too many exits and refuse the
kernel after it's built"; and on splitting the functions: "I want inlining under control anyway, so yes I agree
with the fix".

## The mechanism

The G13 back end joins a loop's exits that lead to different code (an early `return`, a `break` out to code the
normal end skips) at one place. It numbers them, and each exit sets its number in a 16-bit half register before it
leaves the loop (`mov_imm r4h, N`). After the join, the code tests that register against each number in turn
(`if_icmp` / `else_icmp r0l, ueq, r4h, imm`). The test's immediate is 8 bits. The numbers are counted across the
whole function. Past 255, the test compares against the number's low byte: code 299 is tested as 43. No test
matches, and the thread leaves the function as though it had returned, with whatever its return register holds.
The G15 (M3) does not do this.

Proof, in order:

1. **Disassembly** (applegpu, Dougall Johnson's G13 tables). The original `lp_loop_run` unit numbered its exits up
   to 637 in `main` and in `lp_piece`, 148 of them past 255. `pp_step` reached 506 (`p1_if_line` inlined at 201),
   84 past 255.
2. **A binary patch.** Moving the codes past 255 to free codes under 256 in the compiled unit, with nothing else
   changed, made the failing tests pass on the M1 Max.
3. **A minimal kernel** (`scripts/dev/metal/exit256.swift`). It makes N inlined calls of a loop with an early
   return and a store after it. At N ≤ 66 it gives the CPU's answer on all three Macs. From N = 67 (269 exits) it
   gives a wrong answer on the M1 Max and the M1, the same wrong value on both, and the right one on the M3.

The M2 (G14, `Apple8` like the M3) is not checked: no M2 host. `is_g13` takes `Apple7` without `Apple8`, so an M2
is treated as unaffected.

## Decision

1. **Every compiled kernel is read on a G13, and refused when a function's exits pass 255.**
   - `rerail_device::agx` (`gpu/device/src/mldb/builtin/metal/agx.rs`) reads a serialized binary:
     - it takes the fat file's GPU slice and the nested GPU Mach-O inside its text section;
     - it walks each function's instructions by applegpu's encodings (`agx_g13_table.rs`, generated by
       `scripts/dev/metal/agx-g13-table.py`, BSD-3 license carried);
     - it finds each 16-bit `mov_imm` of a value from 256 to 0xfff into a half register, where the same function
       tests that register with an `if_icmp` or `else_icmp` (`ueq`) against the value's low byte as an 8-bit
       immediate. An fp16 constant (0x3c00) is above the bound.
   - An instruction the tables do not decode is an error, not a pass.
   - Reading the largest unit takes 21 ms.
   - The read runs on a G13 only. It runs wherever the engine gets a binary for one:
     - a kernel compiled through a store (`compile_archived`): the archive is serialized, read, and refused before
       it is kept;
     - a kernel with no store, and every pipeline of the mldb kernels (`checked_pipeline`): the pipeline is made into
       a temporary archive and read;
     - a split's unit (`unit`), whether newly compiled or loaded from the store;
     - an archive loaded from the store (`make_uncounted`);
     - `kernels ship`: an archive that fails is not shipped.
   - A kernel linking units is checked through its units: each unit's dynamic library is read.
   - A refusal names the function, how many of its exits pass 255, and its highest number. Then:
     - a fixed kernel's compile reports it failed (`kernels compile`, the tests' setup step);
     - any other kernel panics, "is not made".
   - The engine never runs a kernel the G13 would miscompile this way.
2. **Inlining is under control: a function's exits stay well under 256.**
   - The preprocessor:
     - `lp_substitute`, `lp_call_next`, `p1_if_line` and `p1_find_header` are `UKL_ONCE`. Each was inlined at two
       or three sites, and each now compiles once as a unit of its own (0571);
     - a region's growth (`lp_grow`, split out of `lp_alloc`, inlined at 34 sites in `lp_piece`) and a line
       splice's end (`splice_end`, 56 sites) are `UKL_RARE`. Both are rare cases, called and kept in their
       callers' units.
   - The transducer's interpreter and sweep (`run_batch`, `label_sweep`): on the G13 a large body (`UKL_LARGE`: the
     task, the steps, the evaluators, the output) is a call (`UKL_ONCE`).
     - `rerail_ukl::metal_source_for(.., g13)` defines `UKL_METAL_G13` for that device, and `ukl/backend.h` reads
       it.
     - These kernels were already past the limit on the M1 (324 and 372 in `run_batch`, 650 in `label_sweep`), so
       they ran miscompiled with no error. The check found them as soon as it was in place.
     - Elsewhere, 0576 §22 stands: the M3 and the CPU inline large bodies.
   - A shipped AIR library is preprocessed per device family: `<library>.g13.metallib` for a G13, and
     `<library>.metallib` for any other Apple GPU.

## Measured (M1 Max, each function's highest exit number)

|---|---|---|---|
| preprocessor | `lp_loop_run` unit: `main`, `lp_piece` | 637 | 129 |
| preprocessor | `pp_step` unit | 506 | 30 |

| `label_sweep` | `main` | 650 | under 190 |

- The sweep tool specialized the constant at 0. With it, the interpreter's highest is 187, in `run_task_in`; before,
  it was 402 to 807.
- With its large bodies called, `label_sweep`'s back end took 3.9 s, against 26 s inlined.

## Tests

- The preprocessor's evaluation tests pass 47 of 47 on the M1 Max, the M1 (Mac mini), the M3, and the CPU (Linux,
  `RERAIL_GPU=cpu`). Before, 37 of 47 passed on the M1s.
- `rerail-accel`: 101 of 101 pass on the M1 Max and the M3. This includes
  `a_kernel_whose_exits_the_g13_numbers_past_255_is_refused`, which builds a kernel of 10 and of 67 calls, plain and
  as a unit: the 67-call kernel is refused on a G13 and made elsewhere.
- `rerail-device`'s `agx` tests and `rerail-ukl` pass on Linux.
- The transducer's `all_backends_execute_every_verified_instruction_and_mode` passes on the M1 Max.
- The `pp_arena` split test now expects 18 units, 7 deep. It checks that the four new units are units, and that
  `lp_grow` and `splice_end` stay local.

## Found on the way

- `kernels`' tests raced: one test's `release()` cleared the table of made kernels while another test made one. The
  tests now take one lock (`MADE`).
- Where a test depends on missing pack data, the M3 fails the same way, so those failures say nothing about this
  change. The matcher tests `spilled_walks_find_the_cpus_roots` and the three pack tests fail on the M1 Max and the
  M3 alike.
