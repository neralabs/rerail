# Code of Conduct

Rerail is built by a small team that ships often, argues about code constantly, and mostly enjoys it. This page is how
we keep it that way as more people join.

It applies in every project space (the repository, its issues, pull requests and discussions, and any channel the
project runs) and wherever you speak for the project: an official email address or account, or an event you attend on
its behalf.

## How we work

- **Say what you mean.** "This races when two sessions start at once" helps more than three paragraphs of "might be
  worth considering". Being direct about the work is not being rude to the person.
- **Bring evidence.** A failing test, a trace or a benchmark settles an argument. Seniority and volume don't.
- **Argue with the code, not its author.**
- **Be wrong quickly.** When someone shows you're wrong, say so and move on. Nobody keeps score, and the fix is usually
  one commit.
- **Ask the basic question.** Everyone is new to most of this codebase, including the people who wrote it.
- **Banter is welcome.** If only one of you is laughing, it isn't banter: stop.

## Out of bounds

Being blunt about code is fine. None of this is, however it's phrased:

- Going after who someone is rather than what they wrote: their race, gender, sexuality, religion, nationality, age,
  disability, looks, or how long they've been writing code.
- Harassment: contacting someone who has asked you to stop, piling on, or following someone from one place to another.
- Sexual comments, imagery or advances.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as an address or a phone number, without their permission.
- Threats of violence, or encouraging anyone to hurt themselves or others.
- Trolling: posting to get a rise out of people rather than to get something resolved.
- Picking fights that have nothing to do with Rerail. Issues and pull requests are for the work.

## Reporting

Email [opensource@nera.software](mailto:opensource@nera.software) with what happened, where (links help), and who was
involved. You don't need to be the target to report something, or to be sure it crosses a line.

The maintainers review every report promptly and fairly, and keep the reporter's identity private.

## What happens next

Maintainers can edit, hide or remove comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that break this code, and
say why when they can. Beyond that, the response scales with the impact:

| Step | For | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correction | Language or behavior that is unwelcome or out of place | A private note saying what crossed the line and why. We may ask for a public apology. |
| Warning | A single serious incident, or a run of smaller ones | No interaction with the people involved, including the maintainers handling it, for a set period, in project spaces and outside them. Breaking it leads to a ban. |
| Temporary ban | A serious violation, or behavior that keeps going after a warning | No interaction with the project or its community for a set period. Breaking it leads to a permanent ban. |
| Permanent ban | A pattern of violations, harassing someone, or attacking a group of people | Removal from every project space, for good. |

These steps follow the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org)'s enforcement guidelines, which
build on [Mozilla's enforcement ladder](https://github.com/mozilla/diversity).
