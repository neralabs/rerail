Every timer Rerail keeps, and why

193 of the 383 timer sites stay. Each was judged against one question: is there a fact (an event, an exit, a lock's release, an answer) that could decide what this clock decides, without a person waiting longer or learning later? For these, no such fact exists, or using it would cost the software or a person more than the clock does. The report on all timers is the map and the plan.

Why the values are what they are, unless a row says otherwise: A kill grace is long enough for a clean shutdown to finish its writes and short beside the person's wait; a start wait is longer than a healthy start on a loaded machine.

What would let them go later, unless a row says otherwise: Nothing lets a kill grace go: no system says in advance whether a process will obey SIGTERM. Windows has no SIGTERM, so a stop request with an answer (AG18) is the better fact there.

A limit on a request or a child process (79)

What would let them go later, unless a row says otherwise: The party waited on reporting its own failure as an event (a child that exits on its error, a daemon that answers 'busy'), or the wait moving off any path a person waits on.
